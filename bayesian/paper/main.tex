\title{The Complement Rule Is Excluded Middle:\\
  Machine-Checked Probability over Intuitionistic Logic}

\begin{abstract}
Kolmogorov's axioms fix not only an arithmetic of chance but a
\emph{logic} of events, a Boolean algebra, and quantum mechanics
long ago showed that the logic is a modeling choice.  We mechanize,
in Lean~4 over \textsf{mathlib}, what probability becomes when the
event logic is \emph{intuitionistic}: valuations on frames, the
algebras of verifiable propositions.  The complement rule
$v(\neg A) = 1 - v(A)$ holds for every valuation exactly when
excluded middle holds in the algebra, and Dempster conditioning
agrees with Bayesian conditioning exactly when the evidence
satisfies excluded middle: probability's classicality, static and
dynamic, is one logical axiom.  On finite frames every valuation is
a classical mass function on points, in general it splits into
atomic and diffuse parts, and its Boolean core carries a totally
monotone Dempster--Shafer belief function.  The ``slack'' replacing
the complement rule is boundary mass in the measure model, and
removing it from a semidecidable event solves the halting problem:
slack is the price of a computable epistemic state.  A
machine-checked dichotomy delimits the probability-monad question,
the naive Cox statement is unsatisfiable, and the
Acz\'el/H\"older analytic core is built from scratch, in about
4{,}300 \texttt{sorry}-free lines.
\end{abstract}
