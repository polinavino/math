\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we take seriously the Cox--Jaynes reading of
probability as \emph{extended logic}, and
make the extension a construction with the logic of events as a
parameter.  The construction fixes an event algebra (the chosen
logic in algebraic form) and takes as its assignments of degrees of
certainty the valuations: the maps from events to numbers satisfying
monotonicity, normalization, and modularity.  We specify the 
two conditions that qualify
the result as a probability calculus: when events are certain (0 or 1 certainty),
the valuations must reconstruct
the underlying event logic, and the value of the construction at
classical logic must be the classical theory. 

The motivation for developing this extension comes from the 
study of program behaviour in terms of outcome probabilities. 
Many properties of programs are only semidecidable.
However, the Boolean
event algebra underlying Kolmogorov's axioms
imposes the law of excluded middle (and therefore decidability) on the
structure of logical connectives applied to events. This makes it
not a good fit for reasoning about events that are not decidable, such
as whether an arbitrary program halts: insisting on a classical,
slack-free readout for such an event does not decide it, but instead
produces an object we show to be uncomputable.
The extension process 
we propose, when fixing its logic parameter to be intuitionistic,
does not conflict with the underlying event logic of computation
by construction, and is capable of reasoning about the possibility of
non-termination.

Substituting the Heyting event algebra (and therefore intuitionistic logic)
for the Boolean algebra representing the classical logic case
changes only the consequences that excluded middle supplied.  Both
qualifying conditions hold at this parameter value
(Sections~\ref{sec:valuation} and~\ref{sec:hinge}), and classicality
enters only through the complement rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:hinge})
and its dynamic counterpart, the agreement of Dempster and Bayesian
conditioning (Theorem~\ref{thm:cond-hinge}).  The complement rule
degrades to the inequality $v(a) + v(\neg a) \le 1$.  The deficit in
this inequality, for which we re-purpose the term slack, is a new
quantity: the value of the undecided region, given by the boundary
mass in the measure model.  Slack is submodular yet does not
determine the valuation.  The weaker logic also adds the two-gap
decomposition of slack, the atomic/diffuse divide, and a
belief-function reading through $\neg\neg$.  

Everything else holds
verbatim on frames: conditioning, the product rule, Bayes symmetry,
disjoint additivity, total probability over genuine partitions, and
inclusion--exclusion itself.  On
finite frames, every valuation is a classical mass function on the
frame's points.  The product-rule half of Cox's theorem is
independent of the logic while the sum-rule half must be posited, so 
the direct transcription of the classical Cox statement is
unsatisfiable. Every result is machine-checked in
Lean~4 over \textsf{mathlib}.

\paragraph{Toward a monad.}
Section~\ref{sec:monad} lays the groundwork for a categorical
exploration of the construction: $\mathrm{Val}$ is a mechanized
functor on locales, and its countably-presented fragment composes as
kernels, though whether that composition is canonical on valuations
rather than their presentations remains open.
A commutative monad structure, where it exists, would be the
intuitionistic counterpart of the Giry monad and a step toward
compositional semantics for probabilistic programs with
semidecidable events.  One candidate formulation exists: the space
of Scott-continuous valuations is the classifying locale of a
geometric theory~\cite{vickers2008,coquand-spitters2009}.
Deleting its continuity axiom still leaves a geometric theory, whose
points, read classically, are exactly our valuations.  Its multiplication,
however, must integrate against a merely modular valuation, and the
diffuse part of Example~\ref{thm:obstruction} is exactly what such
integration fails to capture.  We offer the formulation as a
target, not a result.
Beyond intuitionistic logic, no ambient category sending a logic to
its probability calculus functorially is evident from our
construction, and this question remains open.


\paragraph{Other open problems and future work.}
A natural extension of our mechanization effort involves
completing the Acz\'el development, and mechanizing the
Dutch-book theorem~\cite{weatherson2003}. Both require substantial 
additional effort and were deemed out of scope for this work. 
To extend our mechanization effort with preliminary monad 
results, we would quotient the countable
presentations (Proposition~\ref{prop:mixcountable}) into a Kleisli
category of countably-presented kernels, and mechanize the
classifying-locale formulation (which needs classifying locales of
geometric theories, infrastructure \textsf{mathlib} currently lacks).

Some related mathematical problems remain open as well.
The first is developing a representation of the diffuse part on
non-spatial frames. The second is deriving modularity from a 
criterion more primitive than the
mixture characterization (Theorem~\ref{thm:mixture}), knowing it
cannot come from disjunction data alone
(Example~\ref{thm:no-functional}).

The construction method applies beyond intuitionistic logic:
state the classical desiderata,
delete the axioms the target logic no longer licenses, and check
what survives, what splits, and what becomes unsatisfiable.  Linear
logic is the natural next case: its event algebras are quantales, a
valuation there should grade the consumption of evidence, and the
analogues of our representation theorems and of slack, for the
failure of contraction, remain open~\cite{danos-ehrhard2011}.
